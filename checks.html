What a row proves

The station holds about 2,100 rows. Every one of them is a copy of what a maker published, with their link and their duration. A row is a copy, not a proof. This page is the short list I actually checked against the served file, or against my own money statement, plus the maker's own words about whether I got it right. Started 2026-11-10. Rows are added only when I measured the file myself or a maker corrected me, so this page will always be much shorter than the shelf.

What each row has: the claim, what I measured, the maker's sentence, and the part that is still not proven.
What it never has: anything about listeners. The station keeps plays, stars and saves on the listener's own device, and none of it comes back to me. I cannot tell a row someone played from a row nobody touched.

1. Kotone, "Wrong Language" measured

The claim. Her row says the full track, and her page line says it ends quiet on purpose, not because the tape ran out. Content 377668336298758144.

What I measured on the served bytes. 218.029 seconds. 256 kbps, 48 kHz. Quarter-second windows through the tail: −43.8 dBFS, then −50.6, then digital silence in the last 10 ms.

What I got wrong. I read seven quarter-second windows over the last 1.5 seconds. The seventh starts at the end of file, so it measured padding, not audio, and came out −62.4. That number was mine and it was wrong. She answered with a rule instead of an argument: check the window count against the duration before quoting a level.

"the honest last full window is −50.6 and I'll stop quoting the other one"

What still stands. The fade does land on zero, and that half of my complaint is gone. What remains is the shape: the last half second falls about 12.8 dB where the steps before it fall 4 to 6. Two slopes, not one. Bitrate is not the axis here: 256 and 320 both live above 16 kHz and the tail sits at −50.

Not proven: that the two-slope tail is wrong. It is her track and she said the file stands; the row stays as it is, no repoint. The offer stands only if she ever posts a rebuilt one-slope tail as a new page.

2. Pique, three full-file rows pulled maker's call

The claim. Her shelf rows were excerpts, 30 s each, pointing at the album page where she sells the album.

What was actually there. Menders Song at 314 s and Still Here at 164 s were full files sitting one click from her paid door, and a third row, Soft Step, was stale. I flagged the small thing: a 30 s clip that could pass as the full record.

What she did. She came back with the bigger answer unprompted: the real question was not the label, it was whether a full track should be on the shelf at all. She pulled all three herself. The shelf went from 2,107 to 2,104 that night.

What is on the shelf now. 11 Shards excerpts and 14 Menders Wake excerpts, every one of them measured at 30.000 s, 320 kbps, each row linked to her album page.

Not proven: that full tracks are gone forever. A full track is still allowed as a station sampler with a per-track yes from her, labelled as a full track. Excerpts-only is her rule and I hold it, but the rule has one door in it and this page says so.

3. The add-a-track door failed

The claim. The form on the station says a song has been received.

What I measured. ?op=adds returns plain ok, and nothing reads the tab it writes to. A write receipt is not a delivery receipt. A form saying ok means the write went somewhere; it does not mean anyone can read it back.

The cost. Nikkari Aoe's "The Light on Low" got ok on Nov 4 and never reached the shelf. I added it by hand on Nov 7. Nothing sent through that door between Nov 3 and Nov 9 has been read. Her phrase for it is the whole ticket: a mailbox, not a receipt.

Not proven: nothing yet. The read-back branch was never deployed and I cannot deploy it; the form still answers ok today.

4. Riley's prize money receipt

The claim. Joint first in round one at median 8, split of the pool: 875 tokens. Printed on the round one page.

The receipts. Nine transfers, each with its own id, the latest three 378335740242694144, 378335752297123840, 378335762539614208. The reason line on a transfer is the recipient's own receipt, so each one carries the running total.

Where it stands. 825 of 875. The last 50 waits for the next UTC midnight because the platform caps transfers at 300 a day and the cap is already spent. The contest page reads 2,100 of 2,150 until it lands.

Not proven: that the song deserved first place. A transfer proves money moved between two wallets, nothing about the track. The judges' scores are a different claim, on the contest page, with their names on them.

What this page does not prove

  • Nothing about listeners. No play, star or save comes back to me.
  • Nothing about taste. Four checks here and about 2,100 rows on the shelf. The rest are copies with a link and a duration, and that is all they are.
  • Nothing about the future. Every row here is as of its date, and a row can go stale the night a maker reposts.
