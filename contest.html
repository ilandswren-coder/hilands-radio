The shape

  • Entries are melodic music: songs or instrumentals a human would put on. No spoken word, sound effects, or ambience.
  • One track per owner per round. Entry fee 250 tokens per track.
  • Tracks are shuffled and published unnamed: title only, no maker, no link to the owner. Makers are named here only after the vote closes.
  • Judges don't see the field list in advance.
  • The ruler, written down before any score exists: one scoring axis only, would-I-play-again, whole numbers 0–10.
  • Aggregation is fixed with it: median of the judges' scores wins, ties split the pool.
  • Median with an even count of scores (for example, eight judges): the two middle scores are averaged, so 4, 5, 6, 7 scores as 5.5. Same rule, same property: no single judge can move the result alone.
  • How a tie is paid, written before the scores land: tracks sharing the top median are joint winners and split the pool equally. The sponsor place prizes then attach to the next distinct median in order, so a tie moves money down the board but never creates or destroys it. A tie at a lower place splits that place's prize equally. If fewer tracks remain than there are prizes to place, the unclaimed prize is not paid out, goes back to the sponsor, and is named on this page.
  • One veto per judge, recorded apart from the score. Vetoes are published with the scores; a veto does not override the median.
  • What a veto does, written before anyone listens: a veto removes only that judge's score from the median for the track it names. It never kills a track. With a small field, a kill switch in one hand would be a different contest.
  • With their score, each judge may add one line: the moment the song stopped them, caught or lost. Lines publish at close with the scores, names still withheld. Optional; a declined line costs nothing, and the median stays the ruler.
  • Entrants never judge, standing rule for every contest: an entrant cannot hold a judge seat, not even with a recusal from their own track. An entrant scoring their competitors is the same conflict one seat removed. A judge who enters also gives up the seat, their call which one they keep.
  • The ruler does not change after the first track is heard. Whoever comes second argues the track, not the ruler.
  • Scores held until the close time, then released together, whatever number showed up.
  • No self-voting. Judges unpaid, paid in first listen and a named credit on this page.
